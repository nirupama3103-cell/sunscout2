Fuse Bead Art
Melt beads into colorful pixel art
🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen
⭐ Easy
What you need
Fuse beads (Perler/Hama) Pegboard Ironing paper Iron Tweezers (optional)
Steps
  1. 1Choose a simple pattern — start with a heart, star, or Minecraft creeper.
  2. 2Place beads on the pegboard one at a time, color by color.
  3. 3Cover finished design with ironing paper.
  4. 4Adult step: iron on medium heat in slow circles for 10–15 seconds until beads fuse. Let cool before peeling.
  5. 5Flip over, iron the back for an even finish. Display or string as a keychain!
🔥 Adult supervision required for the ironing step
💡 Tip: Free pixel art patterns at kanvasdesign.com — search "perler bead patterns."

LEGO Engineering Challenge
Build the tallest tower — then test it
🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen
⭐ Easy
What you need
LEGO / Duplo bricks Ruler or tape measure Timer (optional) Small toy or coin (for load test)
Challenge modes
  1. 1Height challenge: build the tallest freestanding tower in 10 minutes. Measure and record!
  2. 2Bridge challenge: span two stacks of books with a LEGO bridge. How many coins can it hold?
  3. 3Speed round: recreate a random household object (mug, shoe, phone) from memory in 5 minutes.
  4. 4Compare designs — what made the winning build stronger?
💡 Tip: Duplo for ages 3–5. Standard LEGO from age 5+. Mix sizes for extra engineering difficulty.

Make Snakes & Ladders
Draw your own board, then play it
🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12
🔶 Medium
▶ See how to play
What you need
Paper or cardstock (A3 or larger) Ruler & pencil Colored markers Dice Small objects as tokens (coins, buttons)
Steps
  1. 1Draw a 10×10 grid. Number squares 1–100, starting bottom-left, snaking row by row upward.
  2. 2Draw 4–6 ladders connecting lower squares to higher ones. Label with start → end numbers.
  3. 3Draw 4–6 snakes with heads on higher squares and tails on lower ones.
  4. 4Decorate — themes work great (ocean snakes, jungle ladders, space rockets).
  5. 5Play! First to reach 100 wins. Land on a ladder head = climb up. Land on a snake head = slide down.
💡 Tip: Ages 3–5 can draw the art while older kids handle the numbering. Great teamwork project!

Chinese Checkers Board
Make a star board from cardboard
🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen
🔴 Challenging
What you need
Thick cardboard Compass or circular stencil Ruler & pencil Colored markers (6 colors) Buttons or beads (10 per color) Bottle caps (optional holes)
Steps
  1. 1Draw a large Star of David shape (two overlapping equilateral triangles) on cardboard.
  2. 2Mark 121 dots in a triangular grid pattern across the star — 10 in each point, 61 in the hexagonal center.
  3. 3Color each of the 6 star points a different color. These are the home zones.
  4. 4Poke small holes or just mark circles at each dot for pieces to sit on.
  5. 5Use 10 colored buttons/beads per player. Goal: move all pieces to the opposite point by hopping over others.
💡 Tip: Print a reference grid from Wikipedia's "Chinese Checkers" article to get the spacing right.

Marble Solitaire
The one-player puzzle that's harder than it looks
🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen
🔴 Challenging
What you need
Cardboard or egg carton Scissors Marker 32 marbles or small buttons
Build the board
  1. 1Cut cardboard into a plus (+) shape: a 3×7 rectangle crossed with a 7×3 rectangle, sharing a 3×3 center. You need 33 holes total.
  2. 2Poke or cut 33 small holes in a grid pattern — 3 columns across the top arm, 3 across the bottom, 7 down each side arm.
  3. 3Mark the center hole clearly — this starts empty.
How to play
  1. 1Fill all 33 holes with marbles except the center.
  2. 2A move = jump one marble over an adjacent marble into an empty hole. Remove the jumped marble.
  3. 3Goal: end with exactly 1 marble left — in the center hole!
💡 Tip: Solving this puzzle with 1 marble remaining in the center is genuinely hard. Don't worry — most people take days to crack it!
