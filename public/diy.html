01

Fuse Bead Art

Melt beads into colorful pixel art

🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen · ⭐ Easy
What you need
Fuse beads (Perler/Hama)PegboardIroning paperIronTweezers (optional)
Photo guide
Steps
  1. 1Choose a simple pattern — start with a heart, star, or Minecraft creeper.
  2. 2Place beads on the pegboard one at a time, color by color.
  3. 3Cover finished design with ironing paper.
  4. 4Adult step: iron on medium heat in slow circles 10–15 seconds until beads fuse. Let cool.
  5. 5Flip over, iron the back for an even finish. Display or string as a keychain!
🔥 Adult supervision required for the ironing step
💡 Free pixel art patterns at kanvasdesign.com — search "perler bead patterns."

LEGO Engineering Challenge

Build the tallest tower — then test it

🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen · ⭐ Easy
What you need
LEGO / Duplo bricksRulerTimer (optional)Small coin (load test)
Challenge modes
  1. 1Height: build the tallest freestanding tower in 10 minutes.
  2. 2Bridge: span two stacks of books. How many coins can it hold?
  3. 3Speed round: recreate a household object from memory in 5 minutes.
  4. 4Compare designs — what made the winning build stronger?
💡 Duplo for ages 3–5. Standard LEGO from age 5+. Mix sizes for extra challenge.
03

Make Snakes & Ladders

Draw your own board, then play it

🧸 3–5 🎒 6–12 · 🔶 Medium
What you need
Paper or cardstock (A3+)Ruler & pencilColored markersDiceCoins or buttons
Steps
  1. 1Draw a 10×10 grid. Number squares 1–100 starting bottom-left, snaking upward.
  2. 2Draw 4–6 ladders connecting lower squares to higher ones.
  3. 3Draw 4–6 snakes — head on a high square, tail on a lower one.
  4. 4Decorate with a theme (ocean, jungle, space!).
  5. 5Play! Ladder head = climb up. Snake head = slide down. First to 100 wins.
💡 Ages 3–5 do the art, older kids handle the numbering. Great teamwork!
04

Chinese Checkers Board

Make a star board from cardboard

🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen · 🔴 Challenging
What you need
Thick cardboardCompass or stencilRuler & pencil6 colored markersButtons (10 per color)
Steps
  1. 1Draw a Star of David (two overlapping equilateral triangles) on cardboard.
  2. 2Mark 121 dots in a triangular grid — 10 in each point, 61 in the center.
  3. 3Color each of the 6 star points a different color — home zones.
  4. 4Poke small holes at each dot for pieces to sit on.
  5. 5Use 10 buttons per player. Move all to the opposite point by hopping over others.
💡 Print a reference grid from Wikipedia's "Chinese Checkers" article to get spacing right.
05

Marble Solitaire

The one-player puzzle that's harder than it looks

🎒 6–12 🛹 Teen · 🔴 Challenging
What you need
Cardboard or egg cartonScissorsMarker32 marbles or buttons
Build the board
  1. 1Cut cardboard into a plus (+) shape — 33 holes total in a grid.
  2. 2Poke 33 small holes in a grid pattern across the plus shape.
  3. 3Mark the center hole clearly — this starts empty.
How to play
  1. 1Fill all 33 holes with marbles except the center.
  2. 2Jump one marble over an adjacent marble into an empty hole. Remove the jumped marble.
  3. 3Goal: end with exactly 1 marble left — in the center hole!
💡 Ending with 1 marble in the center is genuinely hard. Most people take days to crack it!
